01

Five kinds of information

A planning model mixes facts, borrowed parameters and choices. They deserve different levels of trust, so the project keeps them apart.

  • observedRecords about the regionOfficial sown areas 2010–2025, published irrigation limits and plans, upstream runoff.
  • external datasetGridded climateNASA POWER daily weather: reanalysis and satellite data, not station observations.
  • literaturePublished parametersFAO-56 crop coefficients and stages; FAO rice flooding and percolation values.
  • assumedScenario choicesField and canal efficiencies, optimiser corridors, food-crop list, proxy crops.
  • modelledModel outputsEvery water volume, coefficient, saving and water-year class on this site.

In the data files every number carries a status column: real when it comes from an external source about the region or its climate, assumed otherwise. A literature value applied to Kyzylorda Region is recorded as assumed even when the source is cited. The source shows that the value was published, not that it holds for this region.

A high share of “real” values says little about model quality. Overall, 99.7 % of the values in the data files are real, but almost all of them are daily climate records. Every parameter that turns area and climate into a water volume (crop coefficients and calendars, efficiencies, rice parameters) is an assumption: 0 of 88 is measured in the region.

03

Model versus official figures

A long official series of irrigation withdrawal for the region is not publicly available. What exists are single figures: the limit for 2024, the plan for 2025, the limit for 2026 and two partial-season reports. Where a full-season figure exists, the model is compared using the crop areas and climate of the same year.

Across all 36 efficiency and percolation scenarios, the ratio for 2024–2025 ranges from 1.42 to 3.83.

The model is consistently higher than the official figures. The data cannot say why. The documented candidates are:

  1. Area. The model waters every sown hectare (188 094 ha in 2025). By 17 September 2026, 122 000 ha had been irrigated, which suggests that part of the fodder and grain area is irrigated little or not at all.
  2. Crop demand. Modelled ETc is an upper estimate. For rice it is 1.22–1.34 times the literature value.
  3. Definitions. The model computes full demand; a limit or plan is an allocated volume, not a measured withdrawal.
  4. Efficiencies. Field and canal efficiencies are assumptions.

For these reasons the site uses absolute volumes only as modelled estimates under stated assumptions, and puts the weight on differences between crop mixes.

04

Historical crop structures

The region’s crop mix has changed over 2010–2025. This explorer applies the published coefficients to the official areas of any year, under the climate of the same year (or Cmean) and any efficiency scenario. When the year has an official full-season figure and the climate matches, the comparison above is reproduced.

Areas are official statistics; “other grains” is derived as all grains minus rice and wheat. Calculation: the same Σ area × coefficient function the simulator uses, applied to the coefficients computed in Python.

05

Backtest: four checks, not a validation

Four comparisons with independent figures were made. None has more than one observation, so error statistics such as RMSE or correlation are not reported, and no comparison is used to tune the model. The model has not been calibrated.

  • V1 · Partial season, 2025

    n = 1

    5.21 vs 3.03km³, model vs reported, to 4 August 2025 (×1.72)

    The reported figure covers all agricultural withdrawal to 4 August, with an unstated start date. The observation is lower than all 36 efficiency scenarios (4.04–8.65 km³).

    The modelled water at the field to the same date (2.99 km³) is close to the observation. That could point to a different measuring point, to canal efficiency set too low, or to coincidence. With n = 1 these cannot be told apart.

  • V2 · Water per hectare, 2026

    n = 1

    36 347 vs 27 049m³/ha, model vs reported (×1.34)

    The model divides by sown hectares (2025 structure as a stand-in, 2026 climate); the report divides 3.3 km³ by 122 000 irrigated hectares. The denominators differ, so no conclusion is drawn.

  • V3 · Order of magnitude

    n = 1

    3.5–4km³/year “water consumption” (World Bank, years not given)

    The reference lies between the modelled crop consumption (about 2.1 km³) and the modelled river withdrawal (6.5 km³), both averaged over 2010–2018. The source does not define “consumption”.

  • V4 · Rice components

    n = 1 / 1 / 0

    13 431m³/ha modelled rice ETc vs 10 000–11 000 in the literature

    Field supply of 22 200–28 200 m³/ha sits between the “improved” and “existing” literature norms. No observation exists for river withdrawal per hectare of rice.

Agreement with observations has not been established. The direction of the gap (model above observations) is consistent across checks, but its cause (area, efficiency, crop demand or measuring point) cannot be identified from these data.

06

Which assumptions move the result

Each parameter was moved across its documented range with all others at their base values (one at a time). The ranges are assumptions or real year-to-year variation, not measured uncertainty, so the ranking describes sensitivity to the chosen ranges. It does not show which input is the largest real source of error.

  • A pessimistic canal efficiency of 0.40, shown as a scenario and not ranked, raises withdrawal to 10.50 km³ (+43.8 %).
  • Scaling crop demand (ETc) by ±10 % changes withdrawal by ±7.9 %; less than proportionally, because the rice saturation, water layer and percolation terms do not scale with ETc.
  • Precipitation hardly matters: ±20 % changes withdrawal by ∓0.5 %.

The small Kc swing does not mean Kc is unimportant. FAO-56 gives ranges only for some values (end-season Kc of rice, wheat, vegetables and other grains; mid-season Kc of oilseeds). Mid-season Kc of rice and fodder, about 81 % of the 2025 area, the initial values and the climate adjustment were not varied. Withdrawal is nearly proportional to crop demand.

Experimental runs with less sown area

Separately from the presets, the total area was reduced by 5, 10 and 15 %. These runs are labelled experimental. They split the saving into a structure part (the optimum at the same area) and an area part (the additional reduction).

Uncertainty: 2 000 model runs

The one-at-a-time ranking above moves one input while the others stay fixed. Here all ten uncertain inputs move together: 2 000 combinations drawn by Latin hypercube sampling, each run through the same water balance and the same optimiser. Every input is uniform within its range, because the ranges carry no information about shape. That choice is itself an assumption.

The saving stays positive in all 2 000 runs (minimum 1.9 %), but its size depends most on field efficiency (rank correlation 0.74) and rice percolation (0.47). Canal efficiency changes the volume a lot and the percentage saving hardly at all, because it divides every crop’s water by the same number.

This is not a validation. The spread shows how far the answer moves under the stated ranges. It says nothing about which value is true, and it leaves out inputs that were not varied: mid-season Kc of rice and fodder, sowing dates, the crop areas and the model structure.

09

Checking the climate input against a station

The model’s output cannot yet be checked against an independent series of withdrawals (see the backtest). Its climate input can. NASA POWER temperatures were compared with the Kyzylorda weather station (NOAA GSOD 38062, 3 km away) on every day both report, 2010–2025. The station is an independent measurement and was not used to build the model.

The maximum-temperature result is robust. NASA POWER maxima are about 1.6 °C below the station, both on all days and on days when the station reported its maximum explicitly. The minimum-temperature result is not. For 82 % of days the station minimum carries the GSOD flag “*”: it was derived from the synoptic observations instead of being reported, and such minima tend to be too warm. On the explicitly reported days the sign reverses, but those days are mostly in winter, so neither figure can be taken as the typical difference. T3 uses the station minima and inherits this caveat. The confidence intervals come from a block bootstrap over 30-day blocks and cover only the sampling uncertainty of the mean difference. The day boundaries of the two sources differ by about four hours: GSOD days are UTC days, while the NASA POWER files use local solar time (about UTC+4.4 h at 65.5° E). The model itself uses Penman–Monteith, which also depends on humidity, wind and radiation. The station does not report those, so they remain unchecked.

10

Known limitations

Data

  • Climate is a gridded product for one cell, not station observations; recent values may be preliminary.
  • No measured canal or field efficiencies, rice percolation or local crop coefficients.
  • The composition of the fodder group is unknown; alfalfa describes all of it, which probably overstates its water use.
  • No district-level crop or water data; the region is one unit.
  • Only five years of basin runoff; no gauged inflow to the region.
  • No multi-year official series of irrigation withdrawal.

Model

  • Full crop demand is assumed: no deficit irrigation, no unirrigated sown area.
  • Leaching of saline land, drainage releases from rice basins, livestock and fish ponds are not included.
  • Fixed sowing dates and stage lengths in every year.
  • Income, costs, markets, rotations and soils are not modelled.
  • Optimised mixes are corner solutions, found once under Cmean and Ebase.
  • Not validated; not a forecast.

The complete, dated list of limitations is maintained in docs/limitations.md.
