The question

One river, one region, eight crops.

Kyzylorda Region irrigates its fields from the lower Syr Darya. Rice, the thirstiest crop in the model, covers more than two fifths of the sown area. CropMix Twin asks a planning question: how much river water does a crop mix need — and how much would a different mix change it?

  • 188 094ha

    Sown area of the eight modelled crop groups in 2025.

    realBureau of National Statistics

  • 43%

    Share of rice in that area in 2025 (81 697 ha).

    realBureau of National Statistics

  • 3.7km³

    Official irrigation water limit for the region in 2024; 3.2 km³ for 2026.

    realRegional news reports

  • 43 800m³/ha

    River withdrawal per hectare of rice; the other seven groups need 24 300–39 200 m³/ha (mean climate 2010–2025, base efficiencies).

    modelledCropMix Twin

Read the volumes as comparisons, not forecasts. For 2024 the model gives 6.87 km³ of river withdrawal against the 3.7 km³ limit (×1.86). The backtest could not confirm absolute volumes, so the model is most useful for comparing one crop mix with another.

Our research

A model built in layers,
with every input documented.

  1. 01

    Crop water demand

    Reference evapotranspiration from the FAO-56 Penman–Monteith equation and daily NASA POWER climate, multiplied by crop coefficients for eight crop groups.

    ETc = ET0 × Kc

  2. 02

    From field to river

    Effective rainfall, field application efficiency and canal losses turn crop demand into the water that must leave the river. Flooded rice adds saturation, standing water and percolation.

    36 efficiency combinations · assumed

  3. 03

    Water-year scenarios

    Upstream Syr Darya runoff for 2020–2024 sorts years into low, average and high water. Five years make this a coarse classification.

    CAWater-Info yearbooks, 2020–2024

  4. 04

    Crop-mix optimiser

    Linear programming finds the mix with the least river withdrawal while total area stays fixed and each crop and the food crops stay within set limits. Income is not modelled.

    Linear programming · limits assumed

  5. 05

    Backtest and sensitivity

    Comparisons with official figures and a sensitivity analysis show where the model is uncertain. The model is not validated.

    0–1 observations per check

The simulator

Try a crop mix. See the water.

The Crop Allocation Simulator runs the same model in your browser. Every result names the climate and efficiencies it was computed under.

  • Map of the region

    Kyzylorda Region with its 2017 district boundaries and the Syr Darya, for orientation. There is no district-level data: the region is modelled as one unit.

  • Edit the crop mix

    Change areas in a table, or paint a schematic mosaic where one cell is 100 ha. Cell positions are illustrative, not real fields.

  • Set the conditions

    Choose a climate year from 2010–2025 or the mean, and one of 36 combinations of field efficiency, canal efficiency and rice percolation.

  • Compare scenarios

    Baseline 2025, your scenario and three optimised mixes, side by side at three levels: crop need, at the field and river withdrawal.

What we found

A new crop mix alone saves little water.

With total sown area kept at the 2025 level, the optimiser lowers modelled river withdrawal by 1.5–5.6 %, depending on how far each crop may move. Across all 612 climate and efficiency conditions the saving ranges from slightly negative to 13.5 %.

  • Efficiency assumptions move the result most

    Within the tested ranges, canal efficiency (0.50–0.65) shifts modelled withdrawal by 1.94 km³ and field efficiency (0.50–0.70) by 1.28 km³ — more than the climate year (1.09 km³) or the crop structure (0.22 km³). The ranges themselves are assumptions.

  • Big cuts need less land

    No mix with the same total area reaches a 10 % cut or the 3.7 km³ limit. In experimental runs, almost all saving beyond 1.5–5.6 % comes from sowing less, not from changing the mix.

  • Not validated

    Each comparison with official figures rests on zero or one observation. The results are modelled planning estimates, not measurements, and income is not modelled.

Every number is labelled. Inputs are marked real when they come from a cited source and assumed when they do not; results are always modelled.
