About the project

A river, a region and a planning question.

Kyzylorda Region grows most of its crops on water diverted from the lower Syr Darya. Each season, the water available and the crops sown have to be matched. CropMix Twin is an open, documented model that shows how the choice of crops changes the amount of river water that matching requires.

The region

Fields along the lower river

Kyzylorda Region lies in the south of Kazakhstan, on the lower course of the Syr Darya before it reaches the Aral Sea. Its climate is arid, with hot, dry and windy summers, and its farming depends on irrigation from the river and its canal network.

Rice dominates the irrigated landscape. Between 2010 and 2025 the region sowed 74 000–91 000 ha of rice each year, alongside fodder crops, wheat, melons, vegetables, oilseeds, potato and other grains. In 2025 the eight crop groups covered 188 094 ha.

The water question

Less water, the same land?

The water reaching Kyzylorda depends on runoff formed far upstream and on how it is shared along the basin. In the five years with published yearbooks (2020–2024), basin runoff ranged from 32.1 to 42.0 km³, between 0.86 and 1.13 of the long-term mean. The region’s irrigation limit fell from 3.7 km³ in 2024 to 3.2 km³ in 2026.

Allocations are set as seasonal volumes, while decisions about what to sow are made in spring, before the season’s weather is known. Of the crops grown here, rice needs the most water per hectare. Losses between the river and the field are thought to be large, although they have not been measured. The structure of the crop mix and the efficiency of the network both shape how far a limited allocation goes.

Planners already weigh these factors. A model that sets them out in one place, with every assumption visible, can help compare options consistently and show where the uncertainty lies.

The question CropMix Twin asks:

If the total sown area stays the same, how much river water could a different crop mix save, and how does that compare with what efficiency, climate and sown area change?

The answer so far, within the model: changing the mix alone saves 1.5–5.6 % under average conditions; the assumptions about canal and field efficiency move the result more than the crop structure does; and larger cuts come mainly from sowing less. These are model results under stated assumptions. They are not measured savings.

Scope

What it does, and what it cannot do yet

Does

  • Computes crop water demand day by day for eight crop groups from 16 years of climate (FAO-56).
  • Turns demand into water at the field and from the river under 36 efficiency scenarios.
  • Finds the crop mix with the least river withdrawal within set limits, and shows how the saving varies across 612 conditions.
  • Compares results with every official figure that could be found, and reports where they disagree.
  • Labels every input as observed, external, literature, assumed or modelled, and links it to a source.
  • Lets anyone test their own crop mix in the browser.

Cannot yet

  • Reproduce measured withdrawal: the model is not validated, and its absolute volumes are about twice the official figures.
  • Say anything about individual districts, farms or fields.
  • Account for income, costs, markets, soils, salinity or rotations.
  • Forecast a coming season or the water that will be available.
  • Represent deficit irrigation, unirrigated sown area, leaching or drainage releases.

Who it is for

A shared reference for planning talks

  • Water management specialists

    Compare the river withdrawal of different crop structures under the same climate and efficiency assumptions, and see how much of a difference rests on the efficiency assumptions.

  • Regional administration

    Discuss crop-structure targets against seasonal limits with explicit ranges instead of single numbers, and see which data gaps matter most.

  • Agricultural cooperatives

    Understand why rice dominates the regional water balance and what shifting part of the area to other crops changes, and what it does not.

What would improve the model most

The sensitivity analysis and the backtest point to the same gaps. The most valuable additions would be measured canal and field efficiencies; the irrigated (not only sown) area by crop; a multi-year series of actual withdrawal for the region; local crop calendars and the composition of the fodder group; and costs and prices, so that income can be weighed against water.

The project

Open, documented, reproducible

CropMix Twin is a student project for the Xylem Global Student Innovation Challenge 2026 (Water Quantity track). It was built in layers. Crop water demand came first; losses, water-year scenarios, the optimiser, the backtest and the sensitivity analysis were each added on top only after the previous layer was tested.

The calculation is written in Python (pandas, NumPy, SciPy); the website is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no framework, and works offline. Two commands rebuild every result and the website data from the data files, and automated tests check the equations against FAO-56 worked examples and check that the browser reproduces the Python results.

Everything is public:

  • source code and data: github.com/Assygat/CropMix;
  • source register with quotations: data/SOURCES.md;
  • limitations, updated with each module: docs/limitations.md;
  • backtest and sensitivity reports: docs/backtest.md, docs/sensitivity.md.
