# CropMix Twin — competition package (English)

Xylem Global Student Innovation Challenge 2026, Water Quantity track. Numbers below are copied from `outputs/`
(generated by `python -m src.run_all`) and carry their status. The model is a planning model, not a field
measurement, and it has not been validated against an independent series of withdrawals.

## 1. Problem and context

Kyzylorda Region (Kazakhstan) farms about 188 000 ha in the lower Syr Darya basin, which drains into what remains of the
Aral Sea. Rice covers 43 % of the sown area and needs the most water per hectare. Water arrives through a shared
international river, and in dry years the region receives less than it plans for. Planners need to know two things:
how much water a crop mix needs, and what to sow when the water is short.

## 2. Research objective

Build a transparent, reproducible model that (1) converts a crop mix and a climate year into irrigation water
demand, from FAO-56 crop evapotranspiration to river withdrawal; (2) finds crop mixes that meet a water budget under
explicit rules; and (3) states its uncertainty and what it cannot know.

## 3. Method in one paragraph

Daily Penman–Monteith ET₀ (FAO-56, NASA POWER climate 2010–2025) × stage-based crop coefficients with the FAO-56
climate adjustment gives crop demand ETc. USDA-SCS effective rainfall turns it into net irrigation need. Rice adds
saturation, a standing water layer and percolation. Field and canal efficiencies convert the need into river
withdrawal: 1 mm on 1 ha = 10 m³. The 2025 crop mix (official statistics) needs **7.30 km³** in an average year at base
efficiencies. Linear programming (SciPy/HiGHS) then chooses crop areas under the water and policy constraints.

## 5. Key results (model outputs, with assumptions)

- **Why changing the mix alone saves little.** With the total area fixed and each crop allowed ±30 %, the best mix
  saves 3.4 % (2.1–5.9 % across 2 000 Monte Carlo runs). Cutting rice frees 1.074 km³, but 77 % of that water is used
  again by the crops that take over the land; fodder alone needs 39 201 m³/ha against 43 822 for rice. The limiting
  rule is the upper limit of the small low-water crops: doubling it raises the saving to 7.7 %. The food-crop minimum
  does not bind.
- **Water-scarcity planning.** With 70 % of normal water and protective rules (each crop keeps ≥ 50 % of its area,
  grows ≤ 30 %, food crops keep ≥ 80 %), the optimiser keeps **74.0 %** of the sown area, against 70 % for a uniform
  cut, which would also break the food rule. That is 7 469 ha more. Each further million m³ keeps 25.5 ha. Below
  64.6 % of normal water (4.718 km³), no plan meets the protective rules. The solver says so and states the shortfall (at 60 %:
  0.335 km³ missing).
- **Trade-off made visible.** In that 70 % plan rice falls to 51 751 ha (−36.7 %). With the assumed yield of 5.17 t/ha,
  rice output falls from 422 to 268 thousand t. A planner sees the price of the water before deciding.
- **Climate stress.** +2 °C raises seasonal ET₀ by 5.5 % and withdrawal by 5.6 %. The observed 2021 season needs 7.4 %
  more than average. Combined stress (+2 °C, −30 % rain, 70 % water) leaves a 34 % shortfall; protective rules then keep
  69.6 % of the area.
- **Uncertainty.** Withdrawal of the 2025 mix: 6.17–8.75 km³ (5th–95th percentile). The biggest driver is canal
  efficiency (ρ = −0.70), then field efficiency, ET₀ and rice percolation.
- **Input check.** NASA POWER daily maxima are 1.6 °C below the Kyzylorda station (n = 5 699 days; the same on the
  4 984 days with an explicitly reported maximum). The minimum-temperature comparison is inconclusive: 82 % of the
  station minima are flagged as derived from synoptic observations, and on the explicitly reported (mostly winter)
  days the sign of the difference reverses. This checks a climate input, not the irrigation model.

## 6. Innovation statement

CropMix Twin turns an FAO-56 water balance into an **explainable allocation tool** for a water-short irrigated region.
Its value lies in transparency more than in complexity. Every number on the site traces to a source and a status
(real / assumed / modelled). The optimiser's answer comes with the rules that decided it, the marginal value of
water, and a clear "no feasible plan" message with the exact relaxation needed, instead of a silently bent constraint.
The same linear programme runs in the browser and in Python and is verified to agree. Uncertainty is quantified, and
validation is reported honestly, n included. The software is a reproducible regional decision-support prototype
(a "digital twin" in the planning sense), not a continuously synchronised operational twin, and it uses no AI.

## 7. Demonstration scenario (3 minutes, uses actual outputs)

1. Open `web/planner.html`. The default setting is 70 % of normal water, protective rules and an average climate.
   KPIs: demand 7.30 km³, available 5.11 km³, plan uses 5.11 km³, area kept 74.0 %.
2. Read "Why this plan": water is binding (25.5 ha per extra million m³). The food minimum binds. The low-water crops are
   at their maximum. Rice and fodder absorb the cut.
3. Move the slider to 55 %. "No plan meets all the rules": the page shows the missing volume and the largest food area
   possible. Switch rules to Flexible: a plan reappears (62.0 % of the area).
4. Back to 70 %, Protective. Set warming to +2 °C and rainfall to −30 %. Demand rises to 7.77 km³ and area kept falls
   to 69.6 %. Point out that this is a sensitivity experiment, not a projection.
5. Scroll to the Pareto curve: area kept against water. The dashed line shows a uniform cut.
6. Section 04: why the original same-land optimiser saves only 3.4 %.
7. Data & Evidence §08–09: the Monte Carlo range and the station check. Close on the limitations.

## 8. Limitations (must be stated with any result)

Not validated (backtest n ≤ 1 per check). Efficiencies, rice percolation and all planning rules are assumptions. Full
crop demand or nothing: no deficit irrigation, no yield response. The region is a single unit, with no canal network
or timing within the season. Warming and rainfall changes are sensitivity experiments. Economic coefficients are
incomplete. Climate is reanalysis data, checked for temperature only. Full list: `docs/limitations.md`.

## 9. Reproducibility

`pip install -r requirements-lock.txt`, then `python -m src.run_all` (about 1.5 min). Then run `python -m pytest`
and `node --test "tests/js/*.test.js"`. Open `web/index.html` directly from disk; no server or build step is needed.

## 10. Presentation outline (8 slides)

2. The question: what to sow when the water is short.
3. Model chain: ET₀ → ETc → effective rain → losses → river withdrawal (7.30 km³ for 2025).
4. Finding 1: same-land restructuring saves 3.4 % (2.1–5.9 %), and why.

7. Trust: data status, the station check, Monte Carlo, the browser solver matching HiGHS, 320 automated tests (241 Python, 79 JavaScript).
